Palsgraf v. Long Island Railroad Co., 248 N.Y. 339 (1928)
New York Court of Appeals
Topic: Negligence — duty of care and foreseeability (proximate cause)

Excerpt:

Railroad guards, helping a passenger board a moving train, dislodged a
package from his arms. The package, which contained fireworks, exploded
when it fell. The shock of the explosion was alleged to have knocked over
scales at the far end of the platform, injuring Mrs. Palsgraf.

Chief Judge Cardozo, writing for the majority, held that the railroad owed
no duty to Mrs. Palsgraf under these facts. Negligence is not actionable
unless it involves the invasion of a legally protected interest, the
violation of a right. "The risk reasonably to be perceived defines the
duty to be obeyed." Because the guards could not reasonably have foreseen
any harm to Mrs. Palsgraf, standing far away, from the seemingly harmless
act of helping a man with a package, there was no wrong to her, and hence
no liability.

Judge Andrews dissented, arguing that a defendant who acts negligently
owes a duty to everyone, and that the question of liability to a
particular plaintiff is one of proximate cause — whether the injury is so
connected to the negligent act, by a natural and continuous sequence, that
the law is willing to hold the actor responsible.
