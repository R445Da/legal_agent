Hadley v. Baxendale, 156 Eng. Rep. 145 (Court of Exchequer 1854)
Topic: Contract damages — foreseeability / remoteness of loss

Excerpt:

The plaintiffs operated a mill that was stopped because of a broken crank
shaft. They contracted with the defendant carriers to take the broken
shaft to an engineer as a pattern for a new one. The carriers delayed
delivery, and the mill remained idle longer than it otherwise would have.
The plaintiffs sued for lost profits during the extra days of stoppage.

The court laid down the rule for recoverable damages for breach of
contract. Damages should be such as may fairly and reasonably be
considered either (1) arising naturally, according to the usual course of
things, from the breach itself, or (2) such as may reasonably be supposed
to have been in the contemplation of both parties, at the time they made
the contract, as the probable result of the breach.

Where the special circumstances under which the contract was actually made
were never communicated by the plaintiff to the defendant, the defendant
could only be held liable for the loss that would generally follow from
breach in the ordinary run of cases. Because the carriers were not told
that the mill would stay idle without the shaft, the lost profits were too
remote and were not recoverable.
